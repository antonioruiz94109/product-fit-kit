# Is this category a fit?

Score tests 1–4 and 7 **pass**, **partial**, or **fail** using manufacturer pages as evidence. Tests 5 (bounded) and 6 (slow churn) are owner scoping decisions, not evidence tests: the model gives an observation, and the owner sets the roster and accepts the refresh pace. Do not ask for dated launch evidence. Any fail on tests 1–3 means **do not build**. Investigate partials before expanding research.

1. **Published:** the brand prints the specification on its own page.
2. **Objective:** it is a number, unit or yes/no fact, rather than a subjective rating.
3. **Comparable:** brands measure it the same way. A shared unit alone is not enough.
4. **Decisive:** buyers actually narrow their choices using it.
5. **Bounded (owner scoping):** the owner sets a roster of roughly 15–30 models for the relevant market.
6. **Slow churn (owner scoping):** the owner accepts the refresh pace, with annual refresh or slower as the target.
7. **Low risk:** an incorrect value causes inconvenience, not harm.

Poor-fit signals include taste-driven decisions, brand-specific test methods, sprawling size/color/SKU combinations, safety-critical choices, and categories dominated by changing prices.

Run `prompts/fit-test.md` with a browsing-capable AI. Ask it for 3–5 manufacturer pages supporting its evidence-based assessments (tests 1–4 and 7). Read those pages yourself. A plausible explanation without evidence is not a pass.

The example configs are starting points, not proof that their categories pass. A good category can still contain a bad filter: drop or demote that field before collecting more products.
